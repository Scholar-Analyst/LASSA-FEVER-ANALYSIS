- ### Project Overview
Lassa fever is an acute viral haemorrhagic disease that remains a major public health concern in Nigeria and other West African countries. The disease is transmitted to humans primarily through contact with food or household items contaminated by infected rodents. It can also spread through direct contact with the bodily fluids of an infected person.
This project focuses on analysing Lassa fever data from 2020 to 2025 to identify trends and patterns in suspected cases, confirmed cases, and reported deaths over time.
The dataset was obtained from Kaggle and analysed using Microsoft Excel and Power BI. Excel was used for data cleaning, including checking for missing values, correcting data formats, and ensuring consistency. Power BI was used for exploratory data analysis, data modelling, and creating interactive visualisations to present the findings.
The main objective of this project is to understand how Lassa fever cases and deaths have changed over the years, identify periods with increased reported cases, and examine the relationship between suspected cases, confirmed cases, and deaths.
The findings from this analysis are intended to provide useful insights into the pattern of Lassa fever outbreaks and support better public health awareness, disease surveillance, prevention, and control strategies.

### Dataset Description
The dataset used for this project was obtained from Kaggle. It contains weekly reported Lassa fever surveillance data covering the period from 2020 to 2025.
The dataset consists of 313 records and 7 columns, providing information on suspected cases, laboratory-confirmed cases, and reported deaths. It also contains date and epidemiological week information, which makes it possible to analyse how Lassa fever cases and deaths change over time.
The dataset was provided in Microsoft Excel format and used for data cleaning and preparation before being imported into Power BI for analysis and visualisation.

Dataset Variables:

1. week_start_date: The starting date of each epidemiological week.
2. week_end_date: The ending date of each epidemiological week.
3. epi_year: The epidemiological year in which the cases were reported.
4. epi_week: The epidemiological week number used to track disease occurrences throughout the year.
5. suspected_cases: The number of suspected Lassa fever cases reported during each epidemiological week.
6. confirmed_cases: The number of cases confirmed through laboratory testing during each epidemiological week.
7. deaths: The number of deaths reported during each epidemiological week.

### Data Cleaning
The Lassa fever dataset was cleaned and prepared using Microsoft Excel before being imported into Power BI. The cleaning process involved checking for missing values, duplicate records, incorrect data types, date formatting, and data consistency.
The dataset contained 313 rows and 7 columns. The initial checks showed that there were no missing values. The date and numerical columns were also checked to ensure they were properly formatted and suitable for analysis.
After completing these checks, the dataset was organised and prepared for further analysis and visualisation using Power BI.

### Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Exploratory Data Analysis was carried out using Microsoft Power BI to examine the Lassa fever dataset and identify trends and patterns in reported cases and deaths from 2020 to 2025.
The analysis focused on the following areas:

1. Yearly Case Analysis: Examined the number of suspected and confirmed cases recorded each year to identify years with the highest and lowest reported cases.
2. Death Analysis: Analysed the number of reported deaths across the years to understand changes in Lassa fever-related fatalities.
3. Weekly Trend Analysis: Examined suspected cases, confirmed cases, and deaths across epidemiological weeks to identify periods of increased disease activity.
4. Case Fatality Rate: Examined the relationship between confirmed cases and reported deaths to understand the proportion of confirmed cases that resulted in death.
5. Comparison of Cases: Compared suspected cases with laboratory-confirmed cases to understand the difference between reported suspected cases and confirmed infections.
6. Trend Analysis: Investigated how Lassa fever cases and deaths changed over the six-year period.

The analysis helped identify important patterns in the dataset and provided a foundation for developing meaningful insights and recommendations.

### Key Insights
The analysis of Lassa fever data from 2020 to 2025 revealed the following findings:

1. High Number of Suspected Cases: A total of 47,814 suspected cases were recorded during the six-year period.
2. Confirmed Cases: The dataset recorded 6,456 laboratory-confirmed cases between 2020 and 2025.
3. Reported Deaths: A total of 979 deaths were reported during the period.
4. Highest Cases in 2024: The year 2024 recorded the highest number of suspected cases (10,087) and confirmed cases (1,311).
5. Highest Deaths in 2023: The highest annual number of deaths was recorded in 2023, with 210 reported deaths.
6. Lowest Cases in 2021: The year 2021 recorded the lowest suspected cases (4,637), confirmed cases (511), and deaths (77).
7. Weekly Case Trends: The highest weekly suspected cases were recorded in epidemiological week 9 of 2024, with 694 cases.
8. Overall Case Fatality Ratio: The ratio of total reported deaths to total confirmed cases was approximately 15.16%.

The findings show that Lassa fever remained a significant public health concern throughout the period analysed. Although reported cases and deaths varied across the years, 2023 and 2024 recorded particularly high numbers. These findings highlight the importance of continuous disease surveillance, early detection, public awareness, and effective prevention strategies.

### Recommendations
Based on the findings from the Lassa fever data analysis (2020–2025), the following recommendations are suggested:

1. Strengthen Disease Surveillance: Public health authorities should improve disease monitoring and reporting systems to detect Lassa fever cases early and respond quickly to potential outbreaks.
2. Increase Public Awareness: Government agencies and health organisations should educate communities about Lassa fever transmission, symptoms, prevention, and the importance of seeking early medical attention.
3. Improve Early Detection and Diagnosis: Healthcare facilities should be equipped with adequate testing facilities to ensure suspected cases are diagnosed and confirmed promptly.
4. Enhance Healthcare Preparedness: Hospitals should improve their capacity to manage Lassa fever patients by providing adequate medical supplies, trained healthcare workers, and appropriate treatment facilities.
5. Promote Environmental Hygiene: Communities should maintain proper sanitation, store food in rodent-proof containers, and reduce exposure to rodents to minimise the risk of infection.
6. Focus on High-Risk Periods: Since the analysis identified variations in cases across epidemiological weeks and years, health authorities should strengthen preparedness and preventive campaigns during periods of increased reported cases.
7. Improve Data Collection and Reporting: Accurate and consistent data collection should be encouraged to support better disease monitoring, research, and public health decision-making.

Overall Recommendation

Government agencies, healthcare institutions, and communities should work together to strengthen Lassa fever prevention, early detection, treatment, and surveillance. These measures can help reduce the spread of infection and minimise deaths associated with the disease.

### Conclusion
This project analysed Lassa fever surveillance data from 2020 to 2025 to identify trends and patterns in suspected cases, confirmed cases, and reported deaths.

Microsoft Excel was used for data cleaning and preparation, while Power BI was used for data analysis and visualisation through interactive dashboards, charts, and graphs.

The analysis revealed a total of 47,814 suspected cases, 6,456 confirmed cases, and 979 reported deaths during the period. The findings showed that 2024 recorded the highest number of suspected and confirmed cases, while 2023 recorded the highest number of deaths.

The project demonstrates how data analysis and visualisation can transform raw health data into meaningful insights. These insights can help improve understanding of disease trends and highlight the importance of early detection, effective surveillance, public awareness, and preventive healthcare measures.

Overall, this project has provided practical experience in data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, data visualisation, and dashboard development while demonstrating the importance of data-driven decision-making in addressing public health challenges.
